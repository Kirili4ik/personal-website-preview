A reference for making an expressive personal website that people can explore and use.

The first concepts came from your material, your references and design judgment. These books and studies were researched afterward to challenge and improve them. They were not the original inspiration.

Two research agents examined empirical HCI work and design books separately. Read coverage is recorded below: a public excerpt or contents page is not a full-book reading.

Start with these.

  1. Samara for the central creative problem: expressive composition with an underlying order.
  2. Lupton for typographic hierarchy across Work and Life.
  3. Nielsen for deciding what appears now and what opens later.
  4. Tuch et al. and Hornof for evidence about first impressions and labeled grouping, with their limits.

Use free excerpts first. A borrowed or purchased book can support the next art-direction pass; no purchases are needed for this PoC.

The reference shelf.

Studies constrain some risks. Books develop visual judgment. Practice articles help apply both. They do not provide a recipe for personal originality.

What the sources cannot decide.

They do not prove that a yucca should lead, that cream and forest green express you, that a paid font is better for this project, or that your friends will remember this site. Those are creative hypotheses. Your own material, preferences and the reactions of the intended audience decide them.

We will not use Miller's 7 plus or minus 2 as a menu limit, Hick's law as a blanket reason to remove hobbies, or screenshot aesthetics as proof that minimalism is best.

A practical review for the next pass

  1. Show a peer the opening briefly. Ask what they noticed and what kind of person they expect to find. This is a qualitative check, not a replication of a millisecond experiment.
  2. Ask them to find a current research artifact, a personal talk and a non-work interest. Observe wrong turns, extra clicks and whether labels predict the destination.
  3. After a short unrelated task, ask what they remember. We want something personal to survive alongside the job title.
  4. Inspect a real phone, keyboard use and zoom. Ask separately about visual appeal and task difficulty.

Compare the rich Salon and the calmer two-chapter study with a small set of peers. Counterbalance viewing order where possible. Use the sessions to diagnose issues, not to claim statistically proven superiority.

Consult Samara for expressive layout, Lupton for hierarchy, Norman for discoverability, and the linked empirical work with its stated limits.
